Liquidations need a buyer. Bidwall escrows one before the crash.

People borrow USDC against tokenized stocks like NVDAx on Kamino. When the stock falls, that collateral has to be sold fast, into a market far thinner than the price suggests. Bidwall lets a capital provider lock USDC into a fixed-price bid that can't be cancelled, so the collateral already has a buyer when it needs one.

The price is reliable. The exit isn't.

Kamino values NVDAx with an oracle price. That tells you what the stock is worth, not how much of it the on-chain market can absorb. When a loan becomes liquidatable, a liquidator repays part of the debt, takes collateral at a small discount (the liquidation bonus), and normally sells it straight away. If that sale would lose more than the bonus, nobody liquidates, and the lender carries bad debt.

Five parties, one transaction

Borrower

Deposited NVDAx, borrowed USDC. Gets liquidated when NVDAx falls far enough. With Bidwall, the borrower also receives any surplus from the fixed bid price.

Kamino

The lender. Decides if a loan is liquidatable, how much debt is repaid and how much collateral is released. Bidwall calls Kamino's own liquidation and never overrides those rules.

Provider

A market maker or fund. Locks USDC in a bid such as "up to 10 NVDAx at $150 until Friday", can't withdraw or reprice until expiry, and receives NVDAx when the bid fills.

Keeper

Spots a liquidatable loan and submits the transaction. Only the keeper named on the market can, and it can't redirect any money.

Curator

Runs the lending market. The hypothesis is that they'd pay a fee to keep buyers on standby. Nobody has confirmed this yet.

NVDAx

The collateral: tokenized Nvidia stock (Token-2022, 8 decimals). Shown in red wherever it moves on this page.

Crash the price and watch what the bid does

A bid only fills if the collateral Kamino releases, priced at the bid, covers the debt being repaid. Drag the oracle price down. The loan below is illustrative but tuned to the demo: a 5% bonus, a $150 bid and 1,500 USDC in escrow.

Watch the provider's value at the oracle price. Below $150 the provider pays more than the oracle says NVDAx is worth, and the borrower keeps the difference. That's the option the provider gives away: the bid fills exactly when the provider would rather not buy. A reservation fee has to pay for it.

A bid's life: locked, then free

A provider creates and funds a bid, then it activates and locks. During the window it can't be cancelled, repriced or withdrawn. Liquidations consume capacity. After expiry the provider takes back what's left. Drag through the week.

The question that decides whether this is a business

Will someone pay enough for a bid that can't be cancelled that a provider will supply it on useful terms? Locked capital has a cost. Liquidations are rare, so trading income alone rarely covers it. Move the numbers and see what reservation fee would close the gap.

The category isn't new: B.Protocol ran funded liquidation backstops on Ethereum. Bidwall's angle is term-locked, asset-specific capacity that anyone can inspect on-chain, wired directly into Kamino's liquidation path for tokenized stocks. If a market maker and a curator both say no, Bidwall is still strong infrastructure research and a credible hackathon submission. It just isn't a validated business.

Ask your Superteam contacts for a concrete answer

"Interesting, keep me posted" tells us nothing. The useful outcomes are an indicative term sheet (price, size, duration, fee), a named decision a curator would make differently, or a clear "no, because liquidations already work". Pick the questions that fit each person.

Kamino and lending people

  • How do xStocks liquidations play out today? Who liquidates, and have any been slow or missed?
  • Does exit depth for large NVDAx or SPYx positions worry the risk team, or cap collateral limits?
  • Would a curator or vault pay a fee for guaranteed buyers? Who holds that budget?
  • Could Kamino ever count committed capacity in its risk settings?

Market makers and liquidators

  • Would you lock USDC for NVDAx at a fixed price for a fixed term? Roughly what discount, size and duration?
  • What yearly reservation fee would make it worth it?
  • Can you hedge NVDAx inventory off-chain, and how quickly?
  • Is "can't cancel during the term" a dealbreaker? What would make it acceptable?

Builders and mentors

  • Is the story clear in 60 seconds? What confused you?
  • Will judges value a real Kamino CPI with an offline replay, or do we need something live?
  • Who should we talk to at Kamino, xStocks/Backed or a market maker?
