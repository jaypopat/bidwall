Bidwall · flow explainer · 3 October 2026

A buyer that can't disappear when a tokenized-stock loan has to be liquidated

Bidwall lets a capital provider lock USDC into a fixed-price, fixed-term bid for NVDAx collateral. When a Kamino loan backed by NVDAx becomes liquidatable, that bid can repay the debt and receive the collateral in a single atomic transaction.

01The problem

On Kamino you can deposit tokenized stocks such as NVDAx as collateral and borrow USDC against them. When the stock falls far enough, the loan becomes liquidatable: someone repays part of the debt and takes collateral at a discount (the liquidation bonus). That "someone" is a liquidator bot, which normally sells the collateral straight away on a DEX to lock in its profit.

That works only if the collateral can actually be sold. Kamino values NVDAx with an oracle price, but the oracle tells you what the stock is worth, not how much of it the on-chain market can absorb. Our 20 September Jupiter quote snapshot (indicative quotes only, nothing executed) showed:

Why it matters: if a large NVDAx position has to be liquidated and the only exit is a thin DEX pool, liquidators may decline (unprofitable) or sell at a large loss. If no one liquidates in time, the lender is left with bad debt. Bidwall's answer is to arrange a buyer in advance, with funds locked, before the crash happens.

Caveat: Kamino doesn't have to dump a whole $1M position in one trade. Partial liquidations and auctions exist. The quote table is a signal worth investigating, not proof that lenders lose those amounts.

02Who's involved

Borrower

Has the loan

Deposited NVDAx and borrowed USDC on Kamino. Gets liquidated if NVDAx falls too far. Under Bidwall, the borrower receives any surplus from the fixed bid price.

Lender

Kamino Lend v2

Decides whether a loan is liquidatable, how much debt can be repaid and how much collateral is released. Bidwall never overrides these rules. It calls Kamino's own liquidation instruction.

Provider

Funds the bid

A market maker or specialist fund. Locks USDC, for example: "up to 10 NVDAx at $150, until Friday." Can't withdraw or reprice until the bid expires. Receives NVDAx when the bid fills.

Keeper

Pulls the trigger

Spots a liquidatable loan and submits the settlement transaction. Bidwall only lets the keeper named on the market do this, and the keeper can't redirect any funds.

Curator / risk team

Possibly pays for it

Whoever runs the lending market. The hypothesis is that they'd pay a fee to have guaranteed buyers on standby. Unvalidated.

Bidwall

The program

Holds escrow, enforces the lock, checks every token movement and makes the whole liquidation all-or-nothing.

03Without vs with Bidwall

Today
  1. NVDAx crashes, and the loan becomes liquidatable.
  2. A liquidator weighs it up: "Can I sell the seized NVDAx for more than I repay?"
  3. With thin DEX depth and a large size, the answer may be no, so no one acts, or someone acts and dumps into the pool.
  4. The loan sits underwater and Kamino risks bad debt.
With Bidwall
  1. Days earlier, a provider locked 1,500 USDC to buy NVDAx at a fixed price.
  2. NVDAx crashes, and the loan becomes liquidatable.
  3. The keeper submits one transaction: the escrow repays the debt and the NVDAx goes to the provider. No DEX sale is needed.
  4. The debt is repaid, the borrower receives any surplus, and the provider holds the NVDAx and can exit slowly.
The core idea: the bid replaces the forced DEX sale. The provider absorbs the price risk and exits on its own schedule, and collateral never has to hit a thin pool during a crash. Funds are escrowed on-chain, so the buyer can't pull out at the worst moment.

04Bid lifecycle

createTerms: market, price, quantity, start, expiry
fundProvider deposits the maximum USDC cost
activateBid becomes live and locked
locked windowNo cancel, no reprice, no withdrawal
settle ×NEach liquidation consumes some capacity
expire → withdrawProvider reclaims unused USDC

Bid terms are explicit integers. Price is stored as USDC atoms per 10⁸ raw NVDAx atoms. NVDAx is a Token-2022 token with a UI multiplier, so "raw units" and "shares on screen" can differ. Every receipt uses raw units. A bid can be filled partially several times until it is exhausted or expires.

05Inside the one transaction

These are the actual numbers from the local demo, a real Kamino loan captured from mainnet and replayed offline with the oracle pushed down from 225 to 150:

Step by step (all one transaction)

  1. The keeper adds Kamino's own refresh instructions so the reserve, oracle and loan data are current.
  2. Bidwall checks the bid is active, inside its time window, called by the authorised keeper, and quoted against the current bid and loan state, which blocks replays.
  3. Up to max_repay USDC moves from escrow to a Bidwall-owned liquidator account. Kamino never gets control of the escrow.
  4. Bidwall calls Kamino's real liquidate_obligation_and_redeem_reserve_collateral_v2. Kamino rejects healthy loans and stale prices on its own.
  5. Bidwall measures what actually happened: the debt went down, the collateral came out, and nothing is stuck in the middle.
  6. It prices the NVDAx actually delivered at the fixed bid price. If that doesn't cover the repayment, the transaction reverts.
  7. NVDAx goes to the provider, the surplus to the borrower, and unused repay budget back to escrow.
  8. Capacity is reduced and an event is emitted. If any check fails, everything rolls back, including Kamino's part.

06When does a bid actually fill?

A fill happens only if bid price × collateral released ≥ debt repaid. Kamino releases collateral worth roughly repayment × (1 + bonus) at the oracle price. In the demo 594.63 / 566.38 ≈ 1.05, so the bonus (net of Kamino fees) was about 5%. That means the bid can fill only when it is no more than about 5% below the oracle.

Who keeps the liquidation bonus?

With an ordinary liquidator, the liquidator keeps the bonus. With Bidwall, the provider pays its fixed price and any amount above the debt goes back to the borrower. The provider earns from buying below where NVDAx later trades, not from the bonus.

It's an option the provider gives away

The bid is used only when NVDAx has crashed and the oracle is near the bid price, which is exactly when the provider would rather not buy. This is the business's central tension. Someone has to pay the provider to keep that option open.

Where Bidwall wins: large liquidations in thin markets, where an ordinary liquidator can't sell the seized collateral profitably. When DEX depth is fine, ordinary liquidators take the loan first and Bidwall isn't needed. Bidwall is the buyer of last resort, not a replacement for liquidators.

08The open question

Will someone pay enough for a non-cancellable liquidation bid that a provider will supply it on useful terms?

Illustration from the brief, not a market quote:

  • $2M locked for a year at an 8% cost of capital costs $160k.
  • At a 3% spread, that needs about $5.3M of fills a year just to break even, before losses and hedging.
  • Liquidations are rare, so trading income alone probably doesn't cover it. A reservation fee paid by someone (the lender, curator or vault) is likely needed.

Precedent: B.Protocol ran funded liquidation backstops on Ethereum, so the category isn't new. Bidwall's specific angle is term-locked, asset-specific capacity that anyone can inspect, plugged directly into Kamino's liquidation path for tokenized equities.

If a market maker and a curator both say "no", Bidwall is still solid infrastructure research and a strong hackathon technical submission. It just isn't a validated business.

09Vetting with your Superteam contacts

The goal of each conversation is a concrete answer, not "sounds interesting". A clear "no, because…" is just as useful. Pick the questions that fit each person.

Kamino / lending people
  • How do xStocks liquidations actually play out today? Who are the liquidators, and have any been missed or slow?
  • Is exit depth for large NVDAx/SPYx positions something the risk team worries about? Does it cap collateral limits?
  • Would a curator or vault (e.g. Sentora/Veda-style) pay a fee for guaranteed liquidation buyers? Who holds that budget?
  • Would Kamino ever recognise committed capacity in its risk settings?
Market makers / liquidators
  • Would you lock USDC for NVDAx at a fixed price for a fixed term? Roughly what discount, size and duration?
  • What reservation fee (annualised %) would make it worth it?
  • Can you hedge NVDAx inventory off-chain (perps, TradFi), and how fast?
  • Is "can't cancel during the term" a dealbreaker? What would make it acceptable?
Builders / mentors
  • Is the story clear in 60 seconds? What confused you?
  • For judges: does a real Kamino CPI plus an offline replay land, or do we need something live?
  • Who should we talk to at Kamino, xStocks/Backed or an MM?
What counts as a signal
  • strong An indicative term sheet: price rule, size, duration, fee.
  • strong A named decision a curator would make differently.
  • weak "Interesting, keep me posted."
  • useful no "Liquidations already work, because X."
